= Method conditions: hurdle and zero-inflated negative binomial models
:toc:
:toclevels: 2

*Status: not implemented.* This is the specification this package is built against. It is
published before any model code, so the code can be checked against it. It sets out what each
method computes, what it refuses, and what it does not claim.

Two methods are covered: `hurdle_nb` and `zinb`. They were proposed in
https://github.com/hyperpolymath/MetaManifold-WebUI/issues/18[MetaManifold-WebUI#18].

*The consumer* is https://github.com/JoshuaJewell/MetaManifold-WebUI[MetaManifold-WebUI]. A
negative-binomial GLM for differential abundance, requested in
https://github.com/hyperpolymath/MetaManifold-WebUI/issues/102[hyperpolymath/MetaManifold-WebUI#102],
is merged on the `hyperpolymath/MetaManifold-WebUI` fork (PR #103) and not yet upstream:
`Differential.fit_nb` and `Differential.differential_abundance` in
`src/analysis/differential.jl`. This document calls that method *the NB GLM*.

- The data contract, offsets and multiple-testing rule of the NB GLM carry over unchanged.
- MetaManifold-WebUI receives only a thin adapter: a route, a panel, configuration keys and a
  dependency pinned by commit. The models, tests and proofs live here.

*Approval.* The method catalogue v1 (`hyperpolymath/MetaManifold-WebUI`, branch
`archive/main-2026-09-29`, `docs/statistics/method-catalogue-v1.md`, line 78) approves no Poisson or negative-binomial model for sequencing data.
These methods, and the NB GLM they extend, therefore count as approved only once the owner
amends the catalogue. Until then this document specifies; it does not authorise.

== Why these models, and why not as a default

Amplicon count tables contain many zeros. A hurdle or zero-inflated model gives those zeros
their own sub-model. That is a modelling choice, not a correction.

Silverman, Roche, Mukherjee & David (2020) describe four processes that produce zeros in
sequence count data. In their simulations:

- simple count models were adequate across all four, even when the true process was unknown;
- zero inflation was suitable only for one zero-generating process, tied to an unlikely set of
  biological and experimental conditions.

Therefore:

* The NB GLM stays the method of record for differential abundance.
* `hurdle_nb` and `zinb` are *alternative fits shown beside it*. Their purpose is to let a user
  see whether a conclusion depends on how zeros are modelled.
* Neither replaces the NB GLM automatically. No model is chosen by a goodness-of-fit statistic,
  and none by which p-value is smaller.

== What these methods are

[cols="1,2,2,3",options="header"]
|===
|Method |Zero part |Count part |Fitted with

|`hurdle_nb`
|binomial GLM, logit link: P(count > 0)
|zero-truncated negative binomial, log link
|R `pscl::hurdle(dist = "negbin", zero.dist = "binomial")`

|`zinb`
|binomial GLM, logit link: P(structural zero)
|negative binomial, log link, mixed with the zero part
|R `pscl::zeroinfl(dist = "negbin")`
|===

For one taxon, let `g` be the group indicator and `s` the size factor, computed by `tss` or
`rle` exactly as for the NB GLM. Both methods use the same formula:

----
hurdle_nb:  count ~ g + offset(log s)  |  g + log s
zinb:       count ~ g + offset(log s)  |  g + log s
----

*Why the size factor enters the two parts differently.*

* In the count part, `log s` is an *offset* on the log scale, as in the NB GLM.
* In the zero part, `log s` is a *covariate*:
** an offset has no meaning on the logit scale;
** sequencing depth is the main driver of zeros that come from sampling. Leaving depth out of
   the zero part would report a difference in depth between groups as a "presence" effect.

*What each zero part means.*

* `hurdle_nb`: the zero part models whether *any* reads were observed. The count part models
  abundance *given* that the taxon was observed. Both parts have a direct reading.
* `zinb`: the zero part is a mixture weight for "structural" zeros. With one sample per unit,
  that weight is separated from the negative binomial's own zeros *only by the shape of the
  negative binomial distribution*. It is never reported as the probability that a taxon is
  absent (see <<not-claimed>>).

== Data accepted

* *Counts.* The same contract as the NB GLM: non-negative integer read counts, samples × taxa,
  with no pseudocounts. Anything else is refused, not rounded.
* *Groups.* Two: a reference and a contrast, as for the NB GLM.
* *Configuration.* The consumer's existing keys `analysis.differential.offset` and
  `analysis.differential.min_prevalence` apply unchanged. The adapter is proposed to add one key,
  `analysis.differential.method`, taking `nb_glm` (the default), `hurdle_nb` or `zinb`.
* *Minimum size.* These are the floors at which a fit is *computable*. They are far below what
  makes inference *adequate*, so any run under 20 samples is flagged in the results table.
+
[cols="1,3",options="header"]
|===
|Method |Floor

|`zinb`
|Fitted jointly, with 6 parameters: intercept and `g` in the count part, θ, and intercept, `g`
and `log s` in the zero part. A run with 6 or fewer samples is refused.

|`hurdle_nb`
|Fitted as two separate models.

* Zero part: 3 parameters, fitted on all samples. It needs at least 4 samples.
* Count part: 3 parameters (intercept, `g`, θ), fitted only on the positive counts. A taxon
  needs at least 3 positive counts in each group, so 6 or more in total. Below that it is
  refused, with the counts named.
|===

== The test reported per taxon, and multiple testing

* *One p-value per taxon.* It comes from a likelihood ratio test of the group effect in both
  parts together, with 2 degrees of freedom:
** the full model is compared with the same model with `g` dropped from both parts;
** the null value of each group coefficient lies inside its parameter space, so the reference
   distribution is the standard χ² with 2 degrees of freedom.
* *Benjamini–Hochberg* is applied across the taxa that produced this p-value. Taxa that were
  refused or failed are excluded from the family, as for the NB GLM.
* *Per-part estimates are descriptive, with no p-values of their own.* These are the count-part
  log fold change and the zero-part log odds ratio, each with its Wald SE. Giving each a p-value
  would add two more testing families from the same data.

== Comparing against the NB GLM: what replaces the Vuong test

MetaManifold-WebUI#18 asked for a Vuong test of ZINB against NB. *It is not offered*, because
the test is invalid here:

* NB is ZINB with the zero weight at its boundary (π = 0).
* So the two models are neither strictly non-nested nor overlapping in Vuong's sense.
* Wilson (2015) shows that the Vuong test is invalid when used this way.

What is offered instead:

* *Intercept-only zero part.* A likelihood ratio test referred to a 50:50 mixture of χ²₀ and χ²₁
  (Self & Liang, 1987) is valid here. It can be added as a per-taxon diagnostic in this
  restricted form only.
* *Zero part with covariates (the default model above).* No test is offered. "No zero
  inflation" is then not a single boundary point: it is the logit going to −∞. This is a
  stated limit, not a gap.
* *Hurdle against NB.* No comparison test is offered.

Whatever is reported is descriptive. It never switches the method of record.

== Provenance written for every run

* The method name.
* The model formulas, exactly as passed to R.
* The offset method.
* The versions of R, `pscl` and `MASS`.
* How many taxa were tested, refused and failed.
* Any warnings R raised during each fit.

== Dependencies

* *R.* `pscl`, called through RCall. In the consumer's `renv.lock` this adds one package: its
  dependency `MASS` is already locked there, and the rest is base R.
** `glmmTMB` was rejected on dependency size: it brings 72 additional packages.
** `unmarked` was rejected for the same reason: it brings 18.
* *Julia.* Production code uses no Julia likelihood. A pure-Julia log-likelihood for each model
  exists only in the test suite, as an independent oracle.

== Evidence planned before the methods count as implemented

None of this exists yet. In order:

. *Known answers.*
** Data simulated from each model with planted coefficients.
** The planted values must fall inside the reported intervals at the stated rate across repeats.
. *Independent reference.*
** The same data fitted directly with `pscl` in R, and evaluated with the Julia oracle
   likelihood.
** Compared: coefficients, SEs, log-likelihood and the LRT statistic.
** Tolerances are recorded in the test.
. *Negative controls.*
** Every refusal in the table above.
** A null simulation, in which the BH-adjusted false discovery rate is reported.
. *Proofs* (Agda, `--safe --without-K`). Each is labelled _proved_ only for exactly these
  statements:
** The hurdle log-likelihood separates into a zero part and a count part. Hence the joint MLE
   equals the two separately fitted MLEs.
** The truncated-NB normaliser 1 − f~NB~(0; μ, θ) is positive for μ > 0.
** The ZINB zero probability π + (1 − π)·f~NB~(0) lies in [0, 1].

Whether any estimator recovers the truth is a _tested_ claim, never a proved one.

== Occupancy is out of scope here

MetaManifold-WebUI#18 also asks for occupancy models, with ψ for presence and p for detection.
With one sample per unit, only the product ψ·p is observable:

* Single-visit occupancy (Lele, Moreno & Bayne, 2012) separates ψ and p only through the
  functional form of the link.
* Knape & Korner-Nievergelt (2015) show that this rests on the link assumption, not on the data.
  Estimates are sensitive to the choice of link.

Occupancy will therefore be offered only for designs with *technical or PCR replicates*, at
least two per sample:

* there, the replicates themselves identify ψ and p. With K = 2 replicates, let
  a = P(detected twice) = ψp² and b = P(detected once) = 2ψp(1 − p). Then p = 2a/(2a + b) and
  ψ = a/p²;
* occupancy will get its own conditions document;
* the bundled MiSeq_SOP data has no replicates.

`hurdle_lognormal`, also proposed in #18, is dropped. It has no count model, and `pscl` does
not implement it.

[[not-claimed]]
== Explicitly not claimed

* Neither method is endorsed for sequencing data, and neither is preferred to the NB GLM.
* The `zinb` zero weight is *not* the probability that a taxon is absent from a sample. It is
  identified only through the shape of the negative binomial.
* Wald intervals and χ² reference distributions are asymptotic. Per-taxon fits on tens of
  samples are where they are weakest.
* Neither this document nor the NB GLM has had an independent statistical review.
